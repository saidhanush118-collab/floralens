2. LITERATURE REVIEW / EXISTING SYSTEM

2.1 Background Study

E-commerce architectures have evolved from monolithic server-rendered storefronts to distributed, API-first client-side web applications. Research in electronic marketplaces (Bakos, 1997; Turban et al., 2018) demonstrates that digital intermediaries reduce consumer search costs, increase market transparency, and resolve information asymmetry between buyers and localized sellers.

In perishable goods commerce, modern inventory theory highlights the critical importance of atomic stock decrement and localized dispatch windows to prevent stockouts and spoilage (Chopra & Meindl, 2016). When applied to service reservations, temporal scheduling models (Brucker, 2007) establish that discrete interval allocation algorithms are essential to avoid resource contention across shared human labor and material inventory.

2.2 Traditional Flower Purchasing and In-Person Booking

In traditional Indian floral markets, transactions occur predominantly in open-air physical mandis or neighborhood roadside kiosks. The conventional customer workflow involves:

1. Physical Store Discovery: The customer must physically travel to local market clusters to inspect stem quality, stem count, and floral variety.

2. Subjective Price Bargaining: Stems are sold without printed or standardized price tags. Prices fluctuate unpredictably based on festivals, weather conditions, and perceived customer affluence.

3. Unstructured Cash Payments: Transactions occur via cash or non-invoiced peer-to-peer UPI transfers, leaving the customer without formal invoices, return mechanisms, or freshness guarantees.

4. Unverified Lead Times: For customized bouquets or bridal garlands, customers must place advance requests verbally, with frequent occurrences of delayed handovers or substitute flowers due to stockouts.
