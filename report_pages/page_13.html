2.5 Identified Research and Implementation Gaps

From the literature review and comparative analysis, six critical operational gaps were identified. Each gap is addressed by an explicit architectural design decision in FloraLens:

Table 1: Gaps identified in existing work and FloraLens responses

Gap Identified Gap in Existing Platforms Architectural Response in FloraLens
G1 Separation of flower delivery and event venue decoration into disparate, incompatible platforms. Unified dual-catalog architecture supporting both physical inventory orders and calendar-scheduled service bookings.
G2 Unchecked calendar bookings leading to decorator schedule collisions on peak event dates. Deterministic double-booking prevention engine validating vendor slot availability across MongoDB Atlas bookings.
G3 Single-tier product models that fail to capture bouquet stem volume options. Dynamic variant data model supporting Standard, Deluxe, and Premium tiers with automatic price recalculation.
G4 Lack of self-service fulfillment tools for small independent vendors. Tailored Vendor Portal featuring multi-stage order tracking (Pending ➔ Confirmed ➔ Preparing ➔ Dispatched ➔ Delivered).
G5 Opaque marketplace governance without listing moderation or vendor credentials audit. Dedicated Platform Administrator Portal with KYC review, business registration audit, and catalog moderation.
G6 Poor localization for Indian domestic e-commerce specifications. Native INR (₹) formatting, Indian address schema (pincode, landmark), and Razorpay sandbox simulation.

2.6 How FloraLens Addresses These Gaps

FloraLens systematically eliminates these deficiencies by delivering an integrated, three-tier cloud-backed web platform. By coupling client-side React 19 state synchronization with server-side collision validation in Express 5 and persistent document collections in MongoDB Atlas, FloraLens provides a transparent, deterministic, and dependable commercial marketplace.
